\sect{Thesis}
\textbf{Long-running agent populations can improve collectively through adaptive social dynamics and intelligence transfer.}

Human innovation is an emergent property of long-running social dynamics; new ideas build on knowledge accumulated over generations through teaching, cooperation, and competition between groups.

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Adaptive social dynamics:} have groups compete for finite resources, so that helping one's group is rewarded even if doing so costs an agent resources. Let agents revise how they cooperate, enter and leave swarms, and migrate between groups.
  \item \textbf{Intelligence transfer:} credit agents for the improvement of those they teach so that it is in their interest to spread useful knowledge; use teaching to combine discoveries from different lineages and have agents verify what they are taught before they adopt it.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Our focus is swarm improvement itself.}

Agent swarms have been observed to spontaneously develop social dynamics, but most existing swarms are one-shot, built for a short-horizon objective, or otherwise evolve independently and have a fixed search space.  We let populations run continually, perturb organization and incentives, and measure how these affect what they discover, retain, and improve on, to identify what sustains collective improvement.
