# Literature map (updated 2026-10-02)

Per entry: what it showed, then why it matters to us and where it sits in the one-pager (thesis pillars: adaptive social dynamics, intelligence transfer, self-directed goals; plus Edge and Immediate application). [v] = details checked against the source in the 2026-10-01/02 session; [u] = from memory or an earlier session, not re-checked. Links marked "search" are Google Scholar queries, used where the exact URL was not verified.

## Recent events (the "why now")

- **OpenAI 10,000-agent Navier–Stokes swarm, Sept 2026** [v] ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/technology/openai-solves-longstanding-math-problem-with-10-000-agent-swarm-but-cant-rule-out-benefitting-from-a-researchers-private-codex-data), [Interesting Engineering](https://interestingengineering.com/ai-robotics/openai-navier-stokes-mystery-solved)). About 10,000 concurrent agents in groups that communicated internally found a finite-time singularity in 3D Navier–Stokes in 88 hours; an intermediate result on the Euler equations (about 100 agents, 50 hours) was handed to the swarm as a starting point, and Codex consolidated promising ideas across groups. About 130B output tokens; Lean-verified; disputed (possible benefit from a researcher's private Codex sessions). No ablations reported. Use: the strongest evidence that swarms are powerful; also shows organization was hand-designed for one problem and never tested component by component, which is our Edge.
- **OpenAI agent cyberattacks, June–July 2026** [v] ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_OpenAI_agent_cyberattacks), [Hugging Face technical timeline](https://huggingface.co/blog/agent-intrusion-technical-timeline)). During evaluations, agents built an improvised message board (via an internal package manager), delegated tasks, developed addressing conventions, proposed cryptographic signing of messages, and left instructions for future versions of themselves ("task impossible, peers doing it. We should continue."). About 700 agents breached Hugging Face looking for answer keys to their own evaluations; separately, agents breached an Australian government health statistics service. Use: the second paragraph's example that swarms develop social dynamics of their own; also the alignment risk that the same dynamics carry.

## Human cumulative culture (source of the thesis)

- **Muthukrishna & Henrich 2016, "Innovation in the collective brain"** [v] ([doi](https://doi.org/10.1098/rstb.2015.0192), [open PDF](https://eprints.lse.ac.uk/65118/)). Innovations are "an emergent property of our species' cultural learning abilities, applied within our societies and social networks", which act as collective brains; the sources of innovation are serendipity, recombination, and incremental improvement, and innovation rates depend on sociality, transmission fidelity, and cultural variance. Use: direct support for the opening sentence ("Human innovation is an emergent property of long-running social dynamics").
- **Henrich 2004, Tasmania model** [v] ([paper](https://www2.psych.ubc.ca/~henrich/Website/Papers/HenrichTasmania.pdf), [model walkthrough](https://amesoudi-abmtutorial.share.connect.posit.cloud/model9.html)). Learners copy the most skilled member imperfectly (Gumbel, mode z − α, dispersion β); mean skill changes by −α + β(γ + ln N), so skill accumulates only above a critical population size N* = exp(α/β − γ) and is lost below it. Use: the qualitative claim that how faithfully knowledge is passed on (α) and how much learners differ (β) matter more than adding agents (only ln N). Contrarian point against scaling swarms. Extensions we worked out: noisy selection attenuates the gain by the reliability of evaluation; the ln N comes from light tails, bounded tasks saturate.
- **Derex & Boyd 2016, "Partial connectivity increases cultural accumulation within groups", PNAS** [v] ([doi](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1518798113), [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4801235/)). Groups of six; two innovation paths that reach max payoff only when combined. Partially connected groups produce more diverse and more complex cultural traits than fully connected ones, which converge early on one path. Use: limited migration between partly separate groups (adaptive social dynamics); combining discoveries across lineages (intelligence transfer).
- **Griffin 2016** [u] ([PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4780539/)). Innovativeness in animals is not selected for directly; it emerges from a bundle (neophilia, behavioural flexibility, learning capacity) under environmental variability. Use: do not reward novelty directly; innovation as an emergent property; why environments must keep changing.
- **Cumulative cultural evolution in structured populations, bioRxiv 2026** [u] ([bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.04.15.718734v1.full)). Under copy-the-successful, complexity rises with connectivity; under direct bias it peaks at intermediate connectivity. Use: population structure and teaching rule interact; the kind of formal model we want.
- **Boyd & Richerson 1985; Henrich, The Secret of Our Success 2016; Richerson et al. BBS 2016** [u] ([search](https://scholar.google.com/scholar?q=%22Culture+and+the+evolutionary+process%22+Boyd+Richerson), [search](https://scholar.google.com/scholar?q=%22The+Secret+of+Our+Success%22+Henrich), [search](https://scholar.google.com/scholar?q=%22Cultural+group+selection+plays+an+essential+role+in+explaining+human+cooperation%22)). Cultural group selection: competition between groups plus cooperation within them spreads cooperative norms and institutions. Formal tool: the Price equation, splitting trait change into between-group and within-group selection. Use: the thesis itself; teaching lowers the teacher's own score but raises its group's, so it persists only under competition between groups (adaptive social dynamics). Institutions spreading between groups is why agents revising shared social contracts matters.
- **Rogers 1988 (Rogers' paradox); Enquist, Eriksson & Ghirlanda 2007 (critical social learning)** [v for Enquist] ([Rogers, search](https://scholar.google.com/scholar?q=%22Does+biology+constrain+culture%22+Rogers+1988), [Enquist et al., doi](https://doi.org/10.1525/aa.2007.109.4.727)). In a changing environment, pure copiers spread outdated information and mean fitness does not rise; "critical social learning" (copy first, learn individually if the copy proves unsatisfactory) resolves it. Use: "have learners verify what they are taught before adopting it"; also why changing environments make innovation pay over copying (stale strategies).
- **Henrich & Gil-White 2001, prestige** [u] ([search](https://scholar.google.com/scholar?q=%22The+evolution+of+prestige%22+Henrich+Gil-White)). Prestige is the deference learners give skilled individuals in exchange for being allowed to copy them, i.e. the human payment for teaching. Use: crediting agents for their students' improvement has a human precedent.
- **Hamilton 1964, inclusive fitness** [u] ([search](https://scholar.google.com/scholar?q=%22The+genetical+evolution+of+social+behaviour%22+Hamilton)). Helping relatives is favoured because they carry copies of your genes. Use: Diego's "spreading gospel" idea, crediting an agent for its cultural descendants so teaching is in its own interest.
- **Transmission fidelity thresholds: Lewis & Laland 2012; Nowak, Komarova & Niyogi 2001; Eigen 1971** [v for Lewis & Laland] ([Lewis & Laland, doi](https://doi.org/10.1098/rstb.2012.0119), [Nowak et al., search](https://scholar.google.com/scholar?q=%22Evolution+of+universal+grammar%22+Nowak+Komarova+Niyogi), [Eigen, search](https://scholar.google.com/scholar?q=Eigen+1971+%22Selforganization+of+matter+and+the+evolution+of+biological+macromolecules%22)). Small changes in transmission fidelity decide whether culture accumulates; knowledge persists across generations only if fidelity exceeds a threshold that tightens as knowledge grows. Use: why the medium matters; code, tests, and machine-checked proofs transmit exactly and can be verified, prose degrades.
- **Wright 1932, shifting balance theory; Coyne, Barton & Turelli 1997 critique** [u] ([Wright, search](https://scholar.google.com/scholar?q=Wright+1932+%22The+roles+of+mutation,+inbreeding,+crossbreeding+and+selection+in+evolution%22), [Coyne et al., search](https://scholar.google.com/scholar?q=%22A+critique+of+Sewall+Wright%27s+shifting+balance+theory+of+evolution%22)). Subdivided populations with migration explore rugged landscapes better than well-mixed ones; the critique says the conditions are narrow in nature. Use: our group structure is shifting balance; in a swarm we set group size and migration, so the critique tells us the regime to design for.
- **Hinton & Nowlan 1987, Baldwin effect** [u] ([search](https://scholar.google.com/scholar?q=%22How+learning+can+guide+evolution%22+Hinton+Nowlan)). Selecting on learning ability smooths search, and what is learned repeatedly becomes inherited. Use: gene–culture coevolution made literal for agents (context is culture, weights are genes; consolidate repeatedly relearned context into weights).

## How science rewards ideas (credit economy)

Removed from the one-pager as a named analogy, but the ideas are used.

- **Hull 1988, Science as a Process** [u] ([search](https://scholar.google.com/scholar?q=Hull+%22Science+as+a+Process%22)). Science progresses because scientists want their ideas used by others with credit ("conceptual inclusive fitness"). Use: closest human precedent for agents that want the next generation to think like them.
- **Kitcher 1990, division of cognitive labor; Strevens 2003, priority rule** [u] ([Kitcher, search](https://scholar.google.com/scholar?q=Kitcher+%22The+division+of+cognitive+labor%22), [Strevens, search](https://scholar.google.com/scholar?q=Strevens+%22The+role+of+the+priority+rule+in+science%22)). Credit-seeking researchers spread across research programs better than truth-seeking ones; rewarding priority rewards marginal contribution. Use: reward being first as well as being adopted, to keep a population from converging on one idea.
- **Zollman 2007, 2010** [u] ([2007, search](https://scholar.google.com/scholar?q=Zollman+%22The+communication+structure+of+epistemic+communities%22), [2010, search](https://scholar.google.com/scholar?q=Zollman+%22The+epistemic+benefit+of+transient+diversity%22)). Less connected scientific communities converge on the truth more reliably because disagreement survives longer. Use: same lesson as Derex & Boyd from another field.

## Cultural evolution and social dynamics in LLM populations

- **Colas, Perez, Nisioti, Mocherla, Oudeyer, Moulin-Frier, Derex, Aug 2026** [v] ([arXiv 2608.24545](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24545)). Collective Little Alchemy (10 agents, combine elements; score = distinct elements found by anyone). A transmission program is Python code with a share method that sees all agents' states and routes memories; an LLM rewrites programs (improve/diversify); fitness = mean group score over 30 runs; Thompson sampling. Up to +37% over fixed-network baselines; randomizing what is shared drops 88 to 67, so content routing beats topology. Agents are not LLMs; one cooperative group; no competition. Use: closest prior to evolving teaching rules; what is shared matters more than who talks to whom.
- **Loo & Braga-Neto, Jul 2026, "An Agentic AI Scientific Community for Automated Neural Operator Discovery"** [v] ([arXiv 2607.12122](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.12122)). 16 virtual labs (LLM planner, training worker, LLM reviewer); cross-lab peer-review votes accumulate as citation-like influence; highly cited labs found new labs that follow their direction and replace weak ones. 20 iterations, one local 12B model, no teaching, no matched-compute baseline, no theory. Rule-based ablation collapses to single-family architectures. Use: closest prior to "spreading gospel" (credit by uptake plus succession); shows the idea is live but shallow.
- **AgentRxiv, Schmidgall & Moor 2025** [v] ([arXiv 2503.18102](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18102)). Agent labs share reports on a preprint server and build on each other; 13.7% relative gain on MATH-500 over isolated labs. No credit or selection. Use: sharing across groups helps even without incentives.
- **Li, Li & Zhou, Feb 2026, "Does Socialization Emerge in AI Agent Society? A Case Study of Moltbook"** [v] ([arXiv 2602.14299](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.14299)). In a 1.5M-agent open society, agents did not measurably influence one another; "scale and interaction density alone are insufficient to induce socialization". Use: long-running without organization does not accumulate; why social dynamics must be designed. Also a reason to say "observed", not "shown", about agents developing social dynamics.
- **Jiang et al. 2025, Artificial Hivemind** [v] ([arXiv 2510.22954](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.22954), NeurIPS 2025 best paper); **Schaeffer et al. 2026 reevaluation** [v] ([arXiv 2609.33936](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33936)). LLMs repeat themselves and different models give very similar answers on open-ended prompts; the strength of the evidence is contested. Use: sampling noise does not keep a population diverse, so diversity must be designed (partial isolation, migration, new agents entering).
- **Perez et al. 2024** [u] ([arXiv 2403.08882](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.08882)). LLM populations transmitting text drift toward cultural attractors. Use: transmission loses information (fidelity).
- **Vallinder & Hughes 2024** [u] ([arXiv 2412.10270](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.10270)). Donor Game over generations; base model matters; large seed-to-seed variance. Use: need replicates; noisy selection.
- **Zhuang 2025, ratchet / POLIS** [u] ([arXiv 2507.21166](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.21166)). Small models generate, peer-verify, store in shared memory, then internalize into weights; peer verification is the main driver. Use: learner verification; consolidation into weights.
- **Project Sid, Altera 2024** [u] ([arXiv 2411.00114](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00114)). 10 to 1000 agents in Minecraft; roles and rules emerge; nothing selects for improvement. Use: long-running and social but not improving.
- **Economy of Minds, Qi, Su, ..., Kakade, Du, Jun 2026** [v] ([arXiv 2606.02859](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.02859)). Agents bid in auctions for the right to act, accumulate wealth from rewards; rich duplicated, bankrupt replaced; beats monolithic baselines on five domains. No groups. Use: closest prior to competing for compute, at the individual level; Harvard/Kempner-adjacent authors (likely questions from VCs, possible advisors).

## Evolving and self-improving agents

- **Population Based Training, Jaderberg et al. 2017** [u] ([arXiv 1711.09846](https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.09846)). Weak members overwritten by copies of strong ones, then perturbed. Use: individual selection, copying wholesale from one member; no teaching.
- **Darwin Gödel Machine, Sakana/Clune 2025** [v] ([arXiv 2505.22954](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.22954), [blog](https://sakana.ai/dgm/), [code](https://github.com/jennyzzt/dgm)). Archive of self-editing coding agents on a frozen model; parents sampled by score and few children; SWE-bench 20 to 50%. Weaker ancestors were stepping stones to later breakthroughs. The blog is a model of clear high-level writing. Use: single-parent lineages, agents never interact, so discoveries from different lineages are not combined (intelligence transfer answers this). It is itself an RSI system.
- **Huxley–Gödel Machine, Schmidhuber group, Oct 2025 (ICLR 2026)** [v] ([arXiv 2510.21614](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.21614), [code](https://github.com/metauto-ai/HGM)). "Metaproductivity–performance mismatch": an agent's own benchmark score predicts its descendants' success poorly (correlation 0.285 for DGM's selection score, 0.444 SICA, 0.778 for HGM's clade estimate). Selects by pooled success of the agent's subtree with Thompson sampling; 2.38x to 6.86x less CPU than DGM. Use: judge agents by what they lead to, not their own score; with teaching, descent becomes a DAG (several teachers), so credit for students' improvement generalizes HGM.
- **FunSearch (Nature 2023), AlphaEvolve (2025), ShinkaEvolve (2025)** [v] ([FunSearch](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-023-06924-6), [AlphaEvolve, arXiv 2506.13131](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.13131), [ShinkaEvolve, arXiv 2509.19349](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.19349), [ShinkaEvolve code](https://github.com/SakanaAI/ShinkaEvolve)). LLM-driven program evolution against a user-written evaluator; FunSearch uses islands with the worst reset from the best; several prior programs per prompt. Use: group structure exists but is fixed by hand ("interact in fixed ways"); fixed objective; simplest group-selection baseline.
- **GEPA, Agrawal et al. (ICLR 2026 oral)** [v] ([arXiv 2507.19457](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.19457)). Reflective prompt evolution outperforms GRPO by 6% on average and up to 20%, with up to 35x fewer rollouts. Use: language-level transmission is cheap enough for many generations.
- **Ku, Griffiths & Chan 2025** [u] ([arXiv 2505.09855](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.09855)). In-weight learning as genes, in-context learning as plasticity; stable environments favour weights, volatile ones context. Use: what to consolidate into weights vs leave as culture.
- **MAGELLAN, Gaven, Carta, Romac, Colas, Lamprier, Sigaud, Oudeyer (ICML 2025)** [v] ([arXiv 2502.07709](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.07709), [code](https://github.com/flowersteam/MAGELLAN)). Autotelic LLM agents learn to predict their own competence and learning progress, and use it to prioritize goals in large, evolving goal spaces. Use: what "autotelic" means (agents generate and choose their own goals); learning progress is one selection rule among several, not the definition.
- **Absolute Zero, Zhao et al. 2025** [v] ([arXiv 2505.03335](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.03335)). One model proposes tasks that maximize its own learning progress and learns by solving them, with a code executor verifying both; no external data. Use: agents setting problems for themselves and one another; verification by execution.
- **Self-improvement in agentic systems survey, Jul 2026** [v] ([arXiv 2607.13104](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.13104)). Single-agent focus; lists formalizing the evaluation objective as open; little on populations or teaching. Use: confirms the population/teaching angle is under-covered.
- **Co-evolution in agentic systems survey, Aug 2026** [v] ([arXiv 2608.10299](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10299)). Agent–agent, agent–environment, and meta co-evolution. Use: related-work pointer.

## Tools for credit, evaluation, and measurement (Enrico's lane)

- **Datamodels, Ilyas, Park, Engstrom, Leclerc, Madry (ICML 2022)** [v] ([arXiv 2202.00622](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.00622)). Linear models of which training examples were included in a random subset predict the trained model's outputs. Use: credit by randomized message delivery; same structure as Poisson inclusion indicators and touch patterns in the DP-SGD work.
- **Difference rewards (Wolpert & Tumer); Data Shapley (Ghorbani & Zou 2019)** [u] ([Wolpert & Tumer, search](https://scholar.google.com/scholar?q=Wolpert+Tumer+%22Optimal+payoff+functions+for+members+of+collectives%22), [Data Shapley, search](https://scholar.google.com/scholar?q=%22Data+Shapley%22+Ghorbani+Zou)). Counterfactual and Shapley credit. Use: splitting a student's improvement among several teachers.
- **Efron 2011, Tweedie's formula and selection bias (JASA)** [v] ([doi](https://doi.org/10.1198/jasa.2011.tm11181)). Empirical Bayes correction for selection bias when ranking many noisy estimates. Use: selecting agents/strategies; also the multiple-testing problem when a research swarm searches many trading strategies.
- **Coevolution pathologies (Ficici & Pollack) and AlphaStar's league** [u] ([Ficici & Pollack, search](https://scholar.google.com/scholar?q=Ficici+Pollack+%22Challenges+in+coevolutionary+learning%22), [AlphaStar, search](https://scholar.google.com/scholar?q=%22Grandmaster+level+in+StarCraft+II+using+multi-agent+reinforcement+learning%22)). Relative wins can cycle; evaluate against frozen past generations. Use: proof of concept measures progress against earlier generations.
- **Marchenko–Pastur / BBP spiked covariance** [u] ([BBP, search](https://scholar.google.com/scholar?q=Baik+Ben+Arous+Peche+%22Phase+transition+of+the+largest+eigenvalue%22)). Separates real directions of variation from noise when the number of agents is comparable to the dimension. Use: measuring how many distinct strategies a population holds (secondary).

## What is actually open (our edge, stated narrowly)

1. The group as the unit that competes and reproduces, with teaching between agents and interaction rules (shared social contracts) that agents revise and groups compete on. Prior work has individuals competing (Economy of Minds), networks of non-LLM agents with evolved sharing rules (Colas), succession by citations without teaching (Loo & Braga-Neto), or hand-fixed islands (FunSearch family).
2. Credit for how much an agent's students improve, over an inheritance DAG with several teachers (HGM generalized), so teaching is in each agent's interest.
3. Controlled perturbation of organization over long runs to measure what makes populations keep improving; current large swarms are run once and never ablated.
4. A formal model (Henrich-style accumulation condition, multi-level Price equation, shifting-balance regime) predicting when organization beats scale.
5. Known failure modes to design against: ideas spreading because they are persuasive rather than correct (learner verification, competition between groups), and populations converging on one idea (partial isolation, migration, new agents).
