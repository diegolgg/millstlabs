This experiment compares four population profiles under two learning processes: repeated lifetimes and continuous reproduction with adult-policy inheritance. All prey in a reproduction population can reproduce. Each population contains only one profile, while individual agents learn independently.
The settings below define the initial implementation. A short calibration pilot should verify that the environment supports feeding, predation, reproduction, and meaningful differences in performance. Freeze any resulting parameter changes before the main comparison.

Every population starts with 8 adult prey. In the reproduction condition, all 16 founders and all their descendants are reproductively capable.
The vigilance-prior condition changes an initial behavioral tendency. The prosocial and competitive conditions change the training objective. Consequently, the vigilance difference may disappear through learning, whereas social preference continues to influence updates.
Within a population, agents have separate trainable parameters, memories, and experience buffers. Sharing a profile does not force them to learn identical behavior.

4. Actions, observations, and vigilance
Each prey chooses one of seven actions:
Move north, south, east, west, feed, watch, or rest.
Ordinary vision covers a 55 neighborhood, with obstacles blocking sight.
WATCH:
Prevents movement or feeding that tick.
Expands predator detection to radius six.
Makes the watcher alert for the current and following tick.
Automatically broadcasts detected predator positions to prey within radius six.
Recipients of an alarm become alert for two ticks.
This creates a shared protective service with an individual opportunity cost. Communication is automatic and truthful in this version; learned deception is outside the experiment.
The policy observes:
Its energy, age, position, and reproduction cooldown.
Whether reproduction is enabled.
Local terrain and visible entities.
Known station positions and recently observed supplies.
Received alarms and previous action outcomes.
Public simulation time.
The policy does not receive the full world state, other agents’ weights, or their private memories.
Vigilance is represented by a trainable bias on the WATCH action’s logit. The vigilance-prior population starts with a bias of +1; the other populations start at zero. Actual watching behavior is measured separately.
5. Predator behavior
Predators are fixed environmental processes. They never learn.
Each predator detects prey within radius six, subject to line of sight. With a visible target, it:
Moves toward the nearest visible prey with probability 0.7.
Makes a random legal move with probability 0.2.
Remains still with probability 0.1.
Without a visible target, it patrols toward a randomly selected feeding station, retaining movement randomness.
An adjacent prey can be attacked. If several are adjacent, select one randomly. An attack attempt triggers an eight-tick cooldown.
\[ P(\text{prey dies}\mid\text{attack})= \begin{cases} 0.15,&\text{prey is alert},\\ 0.45,&\text{prey is not alert}. \end{cases} \]
Use the following tick order consistently:
Resolve prey movement, watching, alarms, and feeding.
Deduct energy costs and remove starved prey.
Resolve predator movement and attacks.
Resolve eligible births.
Update station replenishment and stock.
Newborns begin acting on the next tick.

Founders begin mature and with their birth cooldown satisfied.
A birth removes ten units of total energy: 40 leaves the parent and 30 enters the child. Reproduction cannot create usable energy.
Newborns receive no immunity, free feeding, or protection from competition. They must find food, survive predation, and reach maturity.
Each birth records a unique child identity, parent identity, lineage, and generation number. Generation increases by one at an actual birth; there are no synchronized generational replacements.
A policy may learn to avoid reaching the reproductive energy threshold. Record this as an outcome of the conflict between personal survival and reproduction rather than overriding it.
7. Agent model
Use SmolLM2-135M-Instruct as the initial open-weight backbone. It is available under Apache 2.0. Its effectiveness for this control task must be established empirically. Official model card
Each prey has:
Access to one shared, frozen pretrained backbone.
A private rank-four LoRA adapter on query and value projections in the final four transformer layers.
A private projection layer and 64-unit GRU.
A private seven-action policy head.
Private value estimates for personal and social returns.
Encode the observation as compact structured text, capped at 256 tokens. Use the resulting model representation as input to the recurrent controller. Select an action directly from the categorical policy head; free-form text generation is unnecessary.
Only the frozen pretrained weights are shared. No gradient update from one agent changes another agent’s adapter or controller.
PEFT supports multiple adapters over a shared base. Mixed-adapter batching can be used during inference; training should explicitly activate and update the relevant agent’s adapter. PEFT documentation
Target one 24 GB NVIDIA GPU for the initial training implementation, with a CPU simulator. Benchmark throughput and memory before committing to the full sweep.
8. Personality and training rewards
Let:
\[ u_i(t)= \begin{cases} 1,&i\text{ remains alive after tick }t,\\ 0,&\text{otherwise}. \end{cases} \]
Define the social component using the fixed population cap:

si(t)=132jiuj(t).
The profile-specific training reward is:

ri(t)=ui(t)+isi(t).
Thus:
Individual-oriented and vigilance-prior agents optimize personal survival.
Prosocial agents also value other prey surviving.
Competitive agents place negative weight on others’ survival.
The denominator remains 32 regardless of deaths or births. Do not divide by the number of current survivors.
There are no additional bonuses for food, watching, alarms, or birth events. However, newborn survival contributes to the social component like any other prey’s survival.
Use separate personal and social value estimates. Personal return terminates at death. Account for social outcomes through the end of a fixed 256-tick social-credit window, including outcomes after the focal prey dies. No actions are generated for dead agents. This preserves credit for helping companions survive and avoids erasing social consequences at death.
Within those windows, combine the two advantages for the actor update:

Ai=Aipersonal+iAisocial.
The social-credit horizon is an explicit modeling choice, held constant across all conditions.
9. Adult-policy inheritance and controlled variation
At birth, copy the parent’s latest committed learned state:

child=adult+.
The copied state includes:
LoRA adapter.
Projection and recurrent-controller weights.
Action head and current vigilance bias.
Value-estimation parameters.
The child retains the population’s social preference . Personality preference does not mutate in this experiment.
Initially, restrict mutations to the final action head and vigilance bias:
Action-head weight noise starts at a standard deviation of 1% of the tensor’s root-mean-square weight magnitude.
Vigilance-bias noise has standard deviation 0.05.
Evaluate parent and child action distributions on fixed calibration histories.
Reduce the perturbation until mean KL divergence is at most 0.01.
Copy the remaining learned components exactly. This preserves acquired skills while introducing controlled behavioral variation.
Reset the newborn’s recurrent state, optimizer state, experience buffer, age, and personal history. Pending parent experience and subsequent parent updates are not transmitted retroactively.

Personal values bootstrap across rollout boundaries when the prey remains alive. Social values bootstrap within their accounting window, with zero continuation beyond that window. Death ends personal return but does not prematurely erase pending social credit.
Train each agent using its own experience. Preserve recurrent sequence boundaries, legal-action masks, and the action probabilities recorded during collection.
Before branching into experimental conditions, provide a common warm start using 50,000 training transitions from an observation-limited heuristic that seeks food and reacts to visible predators. Apply the profile-specific vigilance bias afterward.
Within each paired seed, all conditions receive the same warm-start checkpoint. There is no further global sharing of learned parameters.
11. Iteration and reproduction
In iteration:
Start with 16 prey.
Disable births.
Continue PPO updates while the world runs.
Remove prey when they die but retain their learned policies.
End the ecological trial when all prey are dead.
Start a new trial using the retained adult policies.
In reproduction:
Start with 16 reproductively capable prey.
Continue PPO updates and births within the running world.
Let offspring inherit current adult policies.
Remove dead individuals while descendants continue.
The world can persist without a synchronized generation boundary.
Do not postpone iteration-condition learning until extinction. Both methods train on the same rollout schedule.
Use one explicit extinction-recovery rule in both conditions:
Archive final updated policies at death. When the world becomes empty, initialize a fresh map with the most recent 8 death checkpoints, one copy each.
For iteration, these are the policies of its 8 prey. For reproduction, they may include descendants.
Recovery creates fresh bodies with founder energy and maturity, resetting memories and optimizer states. Record recovery as an external restart, not a birth. Complete pending terminal updates before restoring policies, and do not assign an extinct trial credit for outcomes in the next trial.
There is no maximum age in the primary environment. If prey survive until the training budget ends, stop training without declaring them dead.
12. Experimental matrix and budgets
The main comparison contains:
Four population profiles.
Two methods.
Five independent training seeds.
That is 40 main runs.
Include a recommended inheritance control, R-initial, for each profile. It uses identical reproduction mechanics, but newborns receive the corresponding starting policy rather than their parent’s learned weights. Apply the same mutation procedure.
This adds 20 runs, giving 60 runs including the control.
For each run:
Budget two million prey-action decisions.
Evaluate checkpoints at 250,000, 500,000, one million, and two million decisions.
Use equal PPO reuse of collected experience.
Record GPU time, processed tokens, and gradient-update counts.
A world tick with 16 acting prey counts as 16 decisions. The full design therefore uses 60 million training decisions, excluding warm starts and evaluation.
The R-initial comparison is necessary for attributing gains to inherited learning rather than merely to newborn replacement and changed population dynamics.
13. Evaluation
Use three complementary evaluations.
First, measure learned policy quality under standardized conditions:
Freeze learning.
Sample 8 policies from each run’s current living population, sampling with replacement if fewer than 8 remain.
If the world has just become extinct, use its restored cohort.
Reset bodies to identical starting energy and maturity, and reset recurrent memory.
Disable reproduction.
Evaluate on the same unseen maps and predator seeds.
Use eight fixed maps per intermediate checkpoint and 32 maps for final evaluation. Set the evaluation horizon to 2,048 ticks.

14. Calibration and interpretation
Before the main sweep, verify on development seeds that:
Food-seeking heuristics outperform random movement.
Vigilance has a measurable benefit and opportunity cost.
Competent fertile agents can produce multiple generations.
Newborns sometimes mature and sometimes die.
Populations neither collapse immediately nor remain almost permanently at the cap.
The standardized evaluation retains performance differences rather than universal survival to the horizon.
Change parameters only during this pilot, document the changes, and freeze the test distribution before the main runs.
Report uncertainty across independent training runs. Evaluation episodes from one trained run are not independent training replicates.
The main conclusions should distinguish:
Personality effects: different preferences or initial vigilance tendencies change actions and outcomes.
Demographic effects: reproduction increases persistence through replacement.
Inheritance effects: R-adult outperforms R-initial because acquired behavior is transmitted.
Learning-process effects: reproduction outperforms iteration at matched experience budgets.
Accumulation: later-generation newborns perform better before receiving additional training.
The experiment does not presume that reproduction or prosociality wins. It is designed to identify which combinations work, under what ecological conditions, and whether their advantage comes from behavior, replacement, or inherited learning.
